RivalLens D2C

An open-source competitive intelligence engine for direct-to-consumer brands that compares a user's own website, offers, social content, and advertising against competitors and produces concise, evidence-backed recommendations on what to improve or test next.

Audience: D2C founders & growth teams Status: Draft MVP: Web + Social + Meta Ads Primary output: Concise action report
ProblemCompetitor research is fragmented and manual.
SolutionNormalize evidence across owned and rival channels.
DifferentiatorCompare competitors directly against the user's brand.
Viability84 / 100

1. Goals

Actionable intelligence

Tell users what competitors are doing, what appears to work, and what to test next.

Direct comparison

Use the user's own D2C site and connected first-party data as the baseline.

Cross-source evidence

Combine site, product, pricing, social, customer feedback, and ad signals.

Concise reporting

Keep reports focused on advantages, disadvantages, working patterns, and next actions.

2. Target Users

  • D2C founders evaluating direct competitors.
  • Growth and performance-marketing teams.
  • E-commerce operators responsible for pricing, offers, and conversion.
  • Brand/content teams studying competitor creative strategy.
V1 is intentionally D2C-only. B2B/SaaS versions should be separate future product variants rather than mixed into this data model.

4. User Stories

As a D2C operator, I want RivalLens to analyze my site so competitor findings are compared against my actual positioning, pricing, products, and offers.

  • User can submit a public D2C website URL.
  • System extracts homepage positioning, products, pricing, offers, bundles, shipping, guarantees, and subscription signals where present.
  • Extracted evidence is stored with source URL and timestamp.
  • Failures produce explicit source-level error states rather than invented data.

As a D2C operator, I want to monitor competitor websites so I can see meaningful product, offer, and positioning changes over time.

  • User can add at least three competitors.
  • System creates historical snapshots and detects meaningful differences.
  • Changes are classified into pricing, offer, product, positioning, or conversion-related events.
  • Every event links back to evidence.

As a growth marketer, I want competitor Instagram and X content normalized against each account's baseline so I can identify unusually strong or weak patterns.

  • Posts are classified by topic, hook, format, CTA, and promotional intensity.
  • Engagement is normalized rather than ranked solely by raw likes.
  • Comments/replies can be clustered into recurring customer themes.

As a performance marketer, I want to compare observed competitor Meta ad concepts so I can identify repeated creative angles and offers worth testing.

  • Ads are classified by format, hook, angle, offer, CTA, landing page, and creative style.
  • Creative longevity is treated as an observational signal, not proof of ROAS.
  • Meta ingestion remains a replaceable connector.

As a founder, I want a short report that tells me what matters without forcing me through a large analytics dashboard.

  • Report contains exactly four primary sections: our advantages, competitor advantages, what appears to be working, and what to test next.
  • Recommendations distinguish observed facts from hypotheses.
  • Each major conclusion includes evidence and confidence.

6. Architecture

Core rule: facts are extracted first, metrics are computed second, and LLMs interpret structured evidence last. The model must never be the system of record.
LayerResponsibility
ConnectorsWebsite, Instagram, X, Meta Ads, and future first-party integrations.
CollectionCrawling, API retrieval, page snapshots, media capture, and scheduled synchronization.
NormalizationConvert heterogeneous inputs into shared evidence schemas.
Evidence storePersist immutable source observations with URL/account identifiers and timestamps.
Analytics enginesPricing, offer, creative, content, product, customer-feedback, and positioning analysis.
Signal engineCombine independent evidence into confidence-scored strategic hypotheses.
Comparison engineCompare user brand strengths/weaknesses against competitors.
Recommendation layerTranslate supported signals into concrete experiments or actions.

Why TypeScript-first

RivalLens is primarily an orchestration, crawling, background-job, API, and web application, so one TypeScript codebase reduces deployment and maintenance complexity. Python should be added only if later versions require custom statistical or machine-learning pipelines that materially benefit from the Python ecosystem.

8. Non-Goals

  • No B2B/SaaS competitive-intelligence model in V1.
  • No unsupported estimates of competitor revenue, ROAS, conversion rate, or ad spend.
  • No bypassing authentication, CAPTCHAs, access controls, or platform restrictions.
  • No claim that observed public patterns prove causal business success.
  • No large dashboard as the primary user experience.
  • No requirement to support every D2C platform or every social network at launch.

9. Success Metrics

  • At least 90% of displayed strategic conclusions link to source evidence.
  • A first competitor report can be produced after adding the user brand and three competitors.
  • Meaningful site changes are separated from low-value DOM noise with an acceptable false-positive rate.
  • Users can understand the main competitive actions in under two minutes.
  • Reports remain concise and prioritize no more than a small set of material actions.
  • Connector failure for one platform does not prevent analysis from other available sources.
